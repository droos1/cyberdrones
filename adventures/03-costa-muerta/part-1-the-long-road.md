# Adventure 3: Costa Muerta — Session One: The Long Road

> **Status: COMPLETED (Session 1).** Covers the Night City send-off, the road south through the Free Fire Zone, the Aldecaldos waystation, and the border crossing — ending on arrival in Tijuana. The Tijuana buyer tour and the Nueva Costa auction are in **`part-2-the-auction.md`**.

## Background

Two weeks since the clinic siege. The droneware is out, the players are healing, Arasaka is consuming itself in an internal investigation that's spilling into the financial press. Lazard is still alive but increasingly cornered — Kojo's network reports he's been pulled from active duty, his accounts frozen pending audit. He is no longer pointing kill teams at the players. He is fighting to stay out of a corporate confessional booth.

The party still has two sealed droneware units. Sitting on them in Night City is a slow-motion mistake — every fixer in the city is starting to ask the wrong questions, and Trauma Team has been polite-but-curious about a depot job from the same week. **It's time to cash out and disappear for a stretch.**

Kojo has the lead. He's been quietly putting out the word south of the border, where the post-Corp-War legal vacuum means a sealed Arasaka R&D black box is a *trophy*, not a *risk*. Buyers have surfaced. The big one happens once a month: **Costa Muerta**, the offshore black-market biotech auction held on the Pacific rig city of **Nueva Costa**. Three days of credibility tour in Tijuana to land a sponsor, then helicopter to the rigs for auction night.

**Core tension:** This isn't a chase any more. The players are sellers, not prey. They have leverage, they have time, they have a road south — and they have two unmarked devices in a cooler that the most dangerous corporation on Earth would set the continent on fire to recover. The pressure now is *what they choose to do with that power*, and which faction they want carrying their card after this is done.

## Adventure Structure

### Part 0: The Send-Off (Night City)

Two weeks of downtime have given the team time to heal, sit on the droneware, and — crucially — *spend money.* Past adventures have been frugal; the team is thin on chrome. With a 100k+ eb sale on the horizon, Kojo's 15k brokerage already on the table, and a credible road ahead, this is the moment to fix that.

**Actions:**
- Kojo pitches the southern run at Suki's — sells them on the trip (no roll, exposition beat)
- Negotiate Kojo's brokerage cut — **Mats's scene** as the Fixer. Default ask: **15k up front + 10% backend.** PERSUASION DV 13 → drop to 10k up front. DV 15 → drop to 5k up front. DV 17 → no cash up front, 12% backend instead. DV 19 → flat 10% backend, nothing up front. (He won't go below 10% on principle — he's putting his name on this)

---

**Stop 1: Dr. Voss's Clinic — Cyberware Install**

Tanaka's Fish Market, same back-room clinic from Adventure 2. The smell is exactly as bad as remembered. Udo is reading a different paperback now (a Russian historical fiction, dog-eared). Voss has changed nothing about the operating room — the same hardened blast door, the same shelving of components, the same near-pristine surgical theater that exists in the worst-smelling building in Night City.

She greets them politely. They are *even* — she does not owe them, they do not owe her. This is cash business now. But the warmth is real.

*"You came back. Good. I have a new espresso machine."*

She charges fair market rate. Standard install times are abbreviated for the team — Voss is fast, and she has favorable opinions of their nervous systems by now. Surgical downtime: 24-48 hours, which they spend recovering on the road during Day 1 of travel. Fredrik can sleep in the cargo van; Mats and Digitz can drive in shifts.

**Suggested cyberware packages** (each PC picks from their list — pick a few; Voss can install up to three pieces in one sitting before the convoy leaves). *The clean equipment lists are in the **Voss's Menu** handout at the back of this document — hand those to the players.*

*For Fredrik (Solo — survivability + lucha + ranged):*
- **Subdermal Armor** (1,000 eb, +SP under clothes — stacks with armor)
- **Big Knux** (100 eb, 2d6 brawling damage — *built for the lucha bout*)
- **Pain Editor** (1,000 eb — ignore Seriously Wounded penalties; he was a near-death revive last session and this is the obvious answer)
- **Smartgun Link** (1,000 eb — works with his Assault Rifle and SMG)
- **Cybereye TeleOptics** (1,000 eb — extends ranged engagement range)
- Optional: **Kerenzikov** (1,000 eb, +2 init) — if he wants more action economy

*For Mats (Fixer — social armor + survivability + driving):*
- **Skinweave** (500 eb premium, +SP everywhere)
- **Tactile Boost** (500 eb — useful in negotiations and driving)
- **Armorjack Businesswear** (2,000 eb — premium SP 13 armored suit, looks like a tailored jacket)
- **Personal AI Driver Interface** (2,000 eb — for the second vehicle; lets the car semi-autonomously evade while he focuses on shooting/talking)
- **Cyberdeck (basic)** (1,000 eb — entry-level backup hacking; he's not Digitz but having his own deck for low-stakes work is operator-grade)

*For Digitz (Netrunner — netpower + emergency survival):*

**Voss's parting note (Pain Editor warning to Fredrik only):** *"This will let you keep fighting through wounds your body is telling you not to. Listen to your body anyway. The cyberware tells you 'fine.' It does not actually fix anything."* Genuine medical advice. Worth remembering at the table.

**Stop 2: Kojo's Yard — Wheels**

A walled compound behind a strip mall in Watson. Kojo runs a side business in clean vehicles — mostly stolen-and-laundered, but with full real paperwork. He has two main options on the floor (pick one) plus a bike for Fredrik.

**The Main Vehicle — pick one:**

1. **The Cargo Van.** Pre-Krash diesel, manual fallbacks (no remote-hijack surface), reinforced cabin, smuggler compartments in the floor for the droneware coolers, firing slits cut into the rear doors. Slow but durable. Carries the Ricochet without anyone asking questions. SP 15, HP 50, Speed 5. *Free with the brokerage deal — Kojo is invested.*

2. **The Sedan.** Sleek corpo-grade four-door, tinted glass, registered to a real shell company. Arriving at Tijuana buyer meetings in this is a status signal — Mats's Fixer instincts will love it. Faster and quieter than the van but a fraction of the cargo. The Ricochet does not fit cleanly; they'd have to break it down into the trunk and reassemble at the waystation. SP 11, HP 30, Speed 7. *25,000 eb.*

**GM Note — the tradeoff:** Van is the tactical pick (Ricochet, cargo, durability, free). Sedan is the social pick (status at TJ buyer meetings, faster on the road, but Part 2's Ricochet defense becomes harder to set up — they're hauling crates out of a trunk under fire). Let them choose. Either choice is fine; the adventure flexes around it.

**Plus — A Dirtbike for Fredrik.** Per his lifepath, Fredrik has dirtbike experience. Kojo has a clean **Roadbike** ready (Cardinal Industries-style off-road model). Fast, agile, can flank in highway combat, can scout ahead, can leave a fight when the fight gets stupid. Speed 8, HP 25, SP 0. Changes road-combat dynamics — Fredrik becomes a mobile flanker rather than rear gunner. **Strongly recommended.** *12,000 eb.*

**Kojo's note on the bike:** *"It's clean. Papers are real, plate is real, the engine has no remote anything. Don't crash it, but if you have to crash it, crash it at someone."*

### Part 1: The Long Road

**Kojo's final brief.** Suki's basement, the night of departure. Kojo has done the rest of the work — buyer list, safehouse in Tijuana with Ofelia Castañeda, contact protocol for the Aldecaldos at the Snake's Nest waystation, helicopter charter for the auction transit.

He hands Mats a small leather notebook. *"My read on the buyers. Don't lose this. Don't read it in front of anyone. And don't show me a copy."* Inside: handwritten notes on each TJ contact, with stars next to who Kojo would trust if it were his neck.

He takes his cut as negotiated (default 15k up front + 10% backend). Cheap given what he's putting on the line.

**The convoy:** Their main vehicle (van or sedan — Mats driving, Digitz shotgun, Fredrik recovering from chrome install in the back through Day 1) and Fredrik's roadbike (parked or trailered for the highway transit, ridden once they hit Free Fire Zone where flanking becomes useful). If they took the sedan, the Ricochet is in the trunk in pieces — they'll need a setup beat at the Snake's Nest before Part 2.

**The route:** Old I-5 south through what used to be San Diego County. The highway is technically intact — the off-ramps are not. Most of it is Free Fire Zone now, a 200-km stretch where the Aldecaldos and Snake Nation run informal toll waystations and *everyone else* is hostile.

**GM Note:** This part should run fast. 15-20 minutes max. The point is establishing the road, the mood, and getting them into the first combat.

---

**The Highway Ambush (Don Bicho's Crew)**

Late afternoon. Long shadows across the cracked highway.  AWARENESS DV 13: carscoming in from side of road.

Behind them, two converted technicals pull onto the highway from a frontage road — light pickups with autocannons welded to the beds and chain-skirt armor on the doors. A third vehicle, a heavy tow-rig with a ram bumper, accelerates from a side road on the right.

This is **Don Bicho's** crew. He hits convoys that bypass the Aldecaldos toll. He doesn't bother negotiating — he assumes you couldn't afford it, which means he'll just take what you have and roll on.

**Vehicle Combat (Cyberpunk RED Chase Rules):**

Run as a chase scene with three pursuers. Goal: lose them or reach the **Snake's Nest** turnoff ~15 km south, where Aldecaldos guns will deter pursuit.

| Vehicle | Type | Stats | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|-------|
| Players' Van *(if chosen)* | Cargo van, reinforced | Speed 5, SP 15, HP 50 | Slow, durable. Mats drives, Digitz shotgun. Firing slits in rear doors |
| Players' Sedan *(if chosen)* | Sleek corpo sedan | Speed 7, SP 11, HP 30 | Faster, lighter, less durable. No firing slits — rear-seat fire means rolling windows down |
| Fredrik's Roadbike | Dirtbike | Speed 8, HP 25, SP 0 | Fredrik deploys once contact starts. Off-road capable — can leave the highway |
| Don Bicho's Tow-Rig | Heavy ram vehicle | Speed 6, SP 18, HP 60, ram 4d6 | Driven by Don Bicho. Wants to pit the main vehicle off the road |
| Technical #1 / #2 | Light pickup with autocannon | Speed 7, SP 11, HP 35 | Crew of 3 each. Gunner does 5d6, ROF 1. Outrun-fast but glass |

**Fredrik on the bike:** Major tactical wrinkle. Fredrik can flank the technicals (their autocannons are mounted, slow to traverse), draw the tow-rig's attention away from the van, or scout ahead for the Aldecaldos sniper position. His Pop-up VHP cyberarm and bike mobility together make him a serious threat to the lightly-armored technicals. This is the first encounter where the bike *changes* the fight rather than just being scenery.

**Don Bicho:** SP 18 (chrome plating), HP 50, Heavy SMG 4d6, Cyberarm with extending mantis blades 3d6 melee. If pulled into a fight on foot, he's *delighted*. He's a sadist.

**Encounter beats:**
- Round 1: Technicals open up at range. Driver check DV 13 to weave; failure means the gunner gets a clean shot
- Round 2: Fredrik (or whoever is back-doors) can return fire from the cargo van. The van has firing slits cut into the rear doors — Kojo prepared for this
- Round 3: Don Bicho closes for the pit maneuver. Counter-driving check DV 15, or take a 4d6 ram hit and a difficult-terrain shake
- Round 4-5: They should be closing on the Snake's Nest turnoff. As they crest a rise, two Aldecaldos snipers on a billboard ahead light up the technicals — first sniper round drops a gunner. Don Bicho pulls off. *"Until next time, gringos!"*

**If the players want to stand and fight:** Fine, but it's costly. Don Bicho will retreat once two of his three vehicles are critical. He doesn't die here — he becomes a recurring road threat for future returns south.

**GM Note:** The Aldecaldos extraction at the end is the *point* of this combat. Players learn (without being told): the Aldecaldos run this stretch, they noticed your trouble, they decided to help, and you now owe them an introduction. Don't lecture them on it — let the rescue do the talking.

**Combat goal:** Make vehicle combat feel different from everything in Adv 1–2. Fast, loud, mostly outside the van, with the urge to engage from cover. Fredrik can use Autofire here, which his sheet supports.

### Part 2: The Snake's Nest (Aldecaldos Waystation)

A converted gas station and motel complex on the old I-5 corridor, ringed by junker trucks turned into walls. Smoke from grilling food. Children running. A working stage where someone is tuning an electric guitar. This place is *alive* in a way Night City rarely feels.

**Arrival.** The Aldecaldos receive them at the gate. The team of snipers that drove off Don Bicho is sitting on a roof, waving lazily. The players' van is escorted in. An old woman in welding goggles brings them water without being asked.

**Meeting Mateo Cruz.**

Mateo is the waystation elder. Mid-60s, snake tattoos up both arms, half-burned face, eyes that miss nothing. He receives them in a converted office with a wood stove and a chess set he's been playing against himself for ten years.

*"You're carrying something hot. I don't need to know what. Kojo said you'd come, and Kojo doesn't lie to me. So we have business."*

**The favor he wants:**

Don Bicho's crew has been growing. They hit the Snake's Nest three weeks ago — light probing attack, lost two raiders, retreated. Mateo's people repulsed it. But Bicho has been recruiting and he'll be back, harder, and probably tonight — because *he just lost face to your van.*

*"You brought him here. You stay until he tries again. You help us put him down. Then I give you a clean passage south, an introduction at the border, and a letter to my cousin Esmé in Nueva Costa. That gets you in front of the right Nomad table."*

**The Players' Choice:**
- Accept: Aldecaldos goodwill, safe passage, an introduction to Tía Esmé (one of the four buyers)
- Lend him the Ricochet — skill check to get idea, or natural?
- Decline: They leave tonight, but the Aldecaldos won't extend hospitality on the way back, and Tía Esmé won't take their meeting cold

**PERSUASION / CONVERSATION:** Mats can negotiate additional terms (DV 15: Aldecaldos contribute fighters to the camp defense Mats can position; DV 17: they throw in fuel and a swap vehicle).

---

**The Camp Defense (Abstracted)**

Sundown. Mateo's scouts report Don Bicho's force closing — three technicals, a stolen mil-spec MRAP, ~20 raiders. Heavier than last time. He brought everything because the players just made him look weak on the highway.

Run this as a **single resolution beat**, not a full combat. The highway chase already filled the vehicle-fight quota; save the table's combat energy for the auction.

**Setup beat (~5 min of tactical talk).** Players choose:
- **Ricochet placement** — north highway approach (open ground, 400m fire lane), south wash (flanking cover), or split between both (-4 effectiveness, doubled coverage)
- **Where each PC anchors** — Fredrik on the wash with his bike for mobility? Mats running coordination from the gate? Digitz jacked in to slave the Ricochet sensors?
- **PERSUASION DV 15 (Mats):** position Aldecaldos fighters where you want them; otherwise Mateo places them

**Then one roll per player:**
- **Fredrik:** best combat skill vs **DV 15** (his anchor holds)
- **Mats:** Tactics, Leadership, or Persuasion vs **DV 13** (Aldecaldos coordination)
- **Digitz:** Interface vs **DV 15** (Ricochet slaved + comms-jam on Bicho's net)

**Tally successes (out of 3):**

| Successes | Outcome |
|-----------|---------|
| 3 | Clean win. No PC injuries. Cati and El Viudo's cousin both fine. Full faction reward |
| 2 | Win, messy. One PC takes a Light Wound (their choice). El Viudo's cousin is among the wounded (sets up the Part 5 hook) |
| 1 | Pyrrhic. One PC Seriously Wounded. Cati is grazed and shaken (lives). Camp holds but Mateo is grim |
| 0 | Camp barely holds. Two PCs Seriously Wounded. Mateo's gratitude is muted — Tía Esmé's family-bond offer in Part 6 requires PERSUASION DV 17 instead of being offered freely |

**Cati.** A 12-year-old Aldecaldos kid runs ammunition during the fight. Optional roleplay beat in the setup minute — do they wave her off, thank her, learn her name? She's brave and she's been doing this since she was eight. Whatever happens to her in the outcome table is the consequence of the dice.

**Don Bicho** survives regardless. He retreats on a hover-cycle stashed in the wash, vowing return. Future antagonist.

**Aftermath:** Camp survives. Mateo embraces each of them in turn. Cati gives Fredrik a small steel pendant her mother made. Mateo provides:
- Letter of introduction to **Tía Esmé Aldecaldo** in Nueva Costa
- A token for **Comandante Castillo** at the border (a Aldecaldos snake-mark coin — Castillo takes the coin, *he doesn't take cash from you*)
- Fuel, a swap of supplies, basic mechanical repair on the van
- The waystation's brand-of-respect: a small snake tattoo, offered free, on a place of their choosing. **GM Note:** If any player takes it, mark a positive faction standing they can call on once per future adventure

### Part 3: La Frontera (The Border)

The old US-Mexico border crossing south of San Ysidro. The Federal infrastructure is rusted out. **Comandante Castillo** runs the checkpoint with about 30 men and a battery of salvaged anti-air. He's not technically a warlord — he was an actual Federal officer who never got the order to abandon his post, so he just kept *operating it*. Now he charges crossing fees.

**Arrival.**

A line of trucks, motorcycles, and one impossibly old yellow taxi. Castillo's men work the line professionally — a kind of feudal bureaucracy. Forms, fees, vehicle search. The whole operation feels closer to a TSA checkpoint than to a smuggler crossing.

---

**At the Window.**

The team reaches Castillo's clerk. Standard interrogation. Vehicle search incoming.

**Resolution options:**

1. **Mateo's coin.** Hand the snake-mark coin to the clerk. The clerk stops what he's doing. *"One moment, please."* He fetches Castillo personally. Castillo is small, weathered, military-formal. He accepts the coin, *salutes the team* (he respects Mateo), waves them through. Vehicle search canceled. *"Welcome to Mexico, señores. Don't make me a liar for letting you in."*

2. **Bribe.** 5,000 eb gets them through with a perfunctory search. PERSUASION DV 13 to keep the search shallow. Failure: search uncovers the droneware coolers, which initiates a separate negotiation (Castillo asks 50,000 eb to let it pass, and *will* fight if refused).

3. **Bluff their way through.** Forged paperwork, corpo cover. PERSUASION DV 17, with -2 if anyone in the party still has obvious chrome-merc presentation. Failure: vehicle search, see #2.

4. **Netrun fake permits.** Castillo's checkpoint runs a sad little local NET arch on the clerk's terminal — Federal-issue, never updated, jacked into the line scanner. Digitz (or any Netrunner) can plant pre-cleared transit permits while the van crawls forward in line. Architecture: 3 floors. Password (DV 8), Wisp (Black ICE, light), File (the permit registry). Net Action DVs as standard. On success, the clerk's scanner pings green, paperwork prints itself, vehicle search waived. *"Huh. You're already cleared. Move along."* On a botched Cloak or a beaten Wisp, the terminal flags an intrusion — Castillo walks out personally and the team has to fall back to coin / bribe / bluff at -2 (he knows something is wrong).
   - **GM Note:** This is a chance for Digitz to actually carry a scene. Run it as a quick 2–3 turn dive, not a full dungeon. If Mats helps with a face-to-face distraction at the window, give Digitz +2 on the Cloak.

5. **Force.** Suicide. Castillo's anti-air takes the van apart at 200m on the south side. *Don't do this.*

**GM Note:** Most groups will use the coin. If they didn't earn the coin (skipped Part 2's defense) they have to bribe, bluff, or run the NET. The netrun option exists specifically so Digitz has a meaningful play and so groups without persuasion stats still have a clean path.

**The Mole (background sighting).** As the van crawls through the line, a Trauma Team van rolls the *other* way — northbound, out of Mexico. No stop, no interaction. If anyone's watching (AWARENESS), the passenger is a face they know: the Mole from Adv 1–2, alive and exfiltrated. No scene. Just confirmation. Carry it into Adventure 4.

### Arrival: Tijuana (Session One → Session Two handoff)

**Crossing in.** Mexico opens up. The first 20 minutes south of the border feels like a dream sequence — old neon storefronts powered by diesel generators, marigold garlands strung between balconies, three different radio stations bleeding into the van's speakers, a sky the color of antiseptic. It is not Night City. Night City is *behind*.

**Day of the Dead is in three days.** The auction is timed for it deliberately — buyers travel for the festival, money moves through the city, and corp security focuses on the wealthier zones. The whole city is in pre-festival mode. Cyber-lucha posters paper every wall. Pan de muerto in every bakery window. A giant inflatable Catrina above the avenida.

> **This is the Session One cliffhanger.** They're across, they're clean, the droneware is intact, and the city smells like marigolds and diesel. **Session Two picks up here** — at Ofelia's safehouse — in `part-2-the-auction.md`.
